# Go-to-Market Plan

A go-to-market (GTM) plan explains how a product reaches its customers: which segment first, with what positioning, through which channels and sales motion, at what price, with what enablement and how success will be measured. Without one, launches become announcements, sales and marketing chase different targets, and companies spread thinly across segments without winning any. A good GTM plan makes choices and says what the company won't do.

This skill builds GTM plans for new products, new segments, new markets and major repositioning, connecting ICP, positioning, pricing, channels, sales motion, enablement, launch and measurement into one plan.

## Operating principles
1. **Pick a beachhead.** Win one segment clearly before expanding.
2. **Match motion to price and complexity.** Product-led, sales-led and partner-led motions suit different deals.
3. **Align every team.** Product, marketing, sales, customer success and partners work from one plan.
4. **Measure leading and lagging indicators.** Pipeline and activation early; revenue and retention later.
5. **Plan to learn.** Early GTM is a hypothesis; build in checkpoints to adjust.

## GTM motions
| Motion | Fits when | Key levers |
|---|---|---|
| Product-led | Low price, quick value, self-serve onboarding | Free trial or freemium, onboarding, in-product upgrades |
| Sales-led | Higher price, complex buying, several stakeholders | SDRs, AEs, demos, proof, procurement support |
| Product-led sales (hybrid) | Self-serve adoption inside larger companies | Usage signals, sales assist, expansion |
| Partner-led | Partners own the customer relationship or distribution | Resellers, integrators, marketplaces, co-selling |
| Community-led | Strong practitioner communities | Content, events, advocacy |

## GTM plan components
| Component | Question |
|---|---|
| Objective | What business outcome, by when? |
| Target segment and ICP | Who first, and why them? |
| Positioning and messaging | Why us, for them? |
| Pricing and packaging | How do they buy, at what price? |
| Motion and channels | How do we reach and convert them? |
| Sales and enablement | What do teams need to sell? |
| Launch plan | How do we introduce it? |
| Customer success | How do customers reach value and expand? |
| Metrics and checkpoints | How do we know it's working? |
| Risks and assumptions | What could break the plan? |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it for new products, entry into new segments or countries, major repositioning, new pricing models and annual planning.

Don't use it to plan a single feature announcement (`product-launch`) or detailed channel execution (the Performance Marketing and Growth Marketing skills).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Product and what's new about it.
2. Business objective and time frame.
3. ICP, positioning and evidence so far.
4. Pricing ideas and economics.
5. Current channels, sales team structure and partners.
6. Budget, headcount and constraints.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Set the objective
Specific and time-bound: "200 paying mid-market finance teams in 12 months at a target ACV."

### Step 2. Choose the beachhead
Score segments on pain, ability to pay, reachability, competition and fit with your strengths. Pick one.

### Step 3. Confirm positioning and messaging
Tailored to the beachhead.

### Step 4. Decide pricing and packaging
Aligned with value and the motion.

### Step 5. Choose the motion and channels
Based on price, complexity and where buyers learn; prioritise two or three channels.

### Step 6. Plan sales, enablement and success
Team roles, qualification, demo flow, training, onboarding and expansion plays.

### Step 7. Plan the launch
Tiered to the importance of the release.

### Step 8. Define metrics and checkpoints
Leading indicators at 30, 60 and 90 days; lagging at two and four quarters; decision rules for adjusting.

### Step 9. Document risks and assumptions
And the evidence that would prove each wrong.

## Beachhead scoring example
| Segment | Pain severity | Ability to pay | Reachability | Competition | Fit | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-market finance teams on NetSuite | 5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 21 |
| Enterprise finance | 4 | 5 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 16 |
| Small businesses | 3 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 2 | 13 |
Illustrative scores (1 to 5); agree criteria and weights with leadership.

## Metrics by stage
| Stage | Leading indicators | Lagging indicators |
|---|---|---|
| First 90 days | Qualified pipeline, trial activation, demo-to-opportunity rate | First closed deals |
| Two quarters | Win rate in segment, sales cycle length | Revenue, ACV |
| Four quarters | Expansion signals, NPS | Net revenue retention, payback |

## Team responsibilities (RACI-style)
| Activity | Product marketing | Product | Sales | Marketing | Customer success |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Positioning and messaging | Owns | Consulted | Consulted | Consulted | Informed |
| Pricing and packaging | Owns with finance | Consulted | Consulted | Informed | Consulted |
| Demand generation | Consulted | Informed | Informed | Owns | Informed |
| Sales enablement | Owns | Consulted | Consulted | Informed | Informed |
| Onboarding and adoption | Consulted | Consulted | Informed | Informed | Owns |

## Assumption register
| Assumption | Evidence today | How we'll test | Decision date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-market legal teams will pay per user | 6 pilot conversations | First 20 deals | Day 90 |
| Community partnerships produce qualified pipeline | One webinar | Three events | Day 60 |
Writing assumptions down turns the plan into a set of experiments the team can learn from.

## 30-60-90 checkpoint questions
- Day 30: Are we reaching the right accounts? Is messaging landing in calls?
- Day 60: Is qualified pipeline building at the planned rate? Where do deals stall?
- Day 90: Are we winning in the beachhead? Should we adjust segment, message, channel or price?

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Several promising segments | Choose one beachhead; keep others as tested hypotheses |
| Product-led product with enterprise interest | Add a sales-assist layer triggered by usage signals |
| New country | Combine with the International SEO market selection approach |
| Limited budget | Fewer channels, deeper execution |
| Early results miss targets | Use checkpoints to diagnose segment, message, channel or product fit |

## Worked example
A company launching an AI contract review tool considered targeting law firms, in-house legal teams and procurement departments at once. Beachhead scoring favoured in-house legal teams at mid-sized technology companies: high pain from contract volume, budget, reachable through legal ops communities and fewer entrenched competitors. The plan used a sales-led motion with a free contract analysis offer, pricing per user with a team minimum, two channels (legal ops community partnerships and targeted LinkedIn) and checkpoints at 30, 60 and 90 days. By the second quarter, win rates in the beachhead justified hiring two more account executives, while law firms stayed a later phase.

## Output format
```
# GTM plan: <product/segment>, <date>
## Objective
## Beachhead choice and scoring
## Positioning and messaging summary
## Pricing and packaging
## Motion and channels
## Sales, enablement and success plans
## Launch summary
## Metrics, checkpoints and decision rules
## Risks and assumptions
```

## Common mistakes
- Targeting every segment at once.
- Choosing a motion that doesn't fit price and complexity.
- Launch plans without enablement or customer success.
- Only lagging metrics, so problems show up too late.
- No documented assumptions to test.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- Which segment has the most pain and the best fit?
- How will buyers discover, evaluate and buy?
- What would make you change the plan at 90 days?

## Self-check before you answer
- Is there one beachhead with a clear rationale?
- Does the motion fit price and complexity?
- Are all teams covered?
- Are leading metrics, checkpoints and assumptions defined?
