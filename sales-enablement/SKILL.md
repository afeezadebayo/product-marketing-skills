# Sales Enablement

Sales enablement gives sellers what they need to win: a clear story, the right content at the right stage, answers to objections, competitive guidance, product knowledge and practice. Many enablement programmes produce piles of content that sellers never use, stored across shared drives, wikis and chat threads, while reps build their own decks the night before calls. Enablement works when it starts from how deals are actually won and lost, fits into the tools sellers already use and is measured by impact on win rates and cycle times.

This skill builds and improves enablement programmes: the core pitch and deck, stage-based content, objection handling, discovery and demo guides, battlecards, onboarding for new reps, training and measurement.

## Operating principles
1. **Start from deals.** Win-loss findings and call recordings show what sellers need.
2. **Fewer, better assets.** A strong deck, demo flow and objection guide beat a hundred documents.
3. **Meet sellers where they work.** Content inside the CRM, enablement tool or chat, not hidden in folders.
4. **Practise, don't just inform.** Role-plays and call reviews change behaviour; slides rarely do.
5. **Measure use and impact.** Track which assets are used in won deals and how win rates move.

## Core enablement kit
| Asset | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Sales narrative and deck | The story from problem to value, built on positioning |
| Discovery guide | Questions that qualify and uncover pain, triggers and criteria |
| Demo flow | A demo built around the buyer's use case, not a feature tour |
| Objection handling guide | Top objections with evidence-based responses |
| Battlecards | Competitor-specific guidance |
| Case studies and references | Proof by segment and use case |
| ROI or value calculator | Quantifies value for the economic buyer |
| Pricing and packaging guide | How to position plans and discounts |
| Email and follow-up templates | Consistent, helpful outreach |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when win rates fall, when ramp time for new reps is long, after repositioning or launches, when sellers ignore existing content and when entering new segments.

Don't use it to define positioning (`positioning`) or for sales operations topics such as territory design and compensation.

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Current deck, demo script, content library and where it lives.
2. Win-loss findings and call recordings (or summaries).
3. Sales process stages and qualification framework.
4. Rep feedback on what helps and what's missing.
5. Ramp time for new reps and win rates by rep, segment and stage.
6. Tools: CRM, enablement platform, call recording.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Diagnose
Where do deals stall or die? Which reps win most, and what do they do differently? Which assets get used?

### Step 2. Build the narrative
A sales story built on positioning: the shift in the world, the problem, the cost of the status quo, the better way, proof, and how to start.

### Step 3. Build stage-based content
Map assets to stages: prospecting, discovery, demo, evaluation, proposal, negotiation, close.

### Step 4. Write objection handling
The top objections from win-loss and calls, with responses and proof.

### Step 5. Design demos
Use-case demos for each priority segment, with a short and a long version.

### Step 6. Organise content
One source of truth, linked from the CRM by stage and deal type; retire outdated assets.

### Step 7. Train and practise
Launch sessions, role-plays, call reviews and certification for new narratives.

### Step 8. Onboard new reps
A 30-60-90 day plan with product, customer, competitor and process learning, plus shadowing.

### Step 9. Measure
Asset use in won and lost deals, win rate, cycle length, ramp time and rep feedback.

## Sales narrative structure
```
1. A shift in the world (why now)
2. Winners and losers of that shift
3. The promised land (what good looks like)
4. Obstacles to getting there
5. How we help (capabilities mapped to obstacles)
6. Proof (customers, results)
7. Next step
```

## Discovery questions (examples)
- What prompted you to look at this now?
- How do you handle this today, and what does it cost you?
- Who else is affected, and who decides?
- What would success look like in six months?
- What have you tried before?
- What would stop you from moving forward?

## Rep onboarding (30-60-90)
| Period | Focus | Proof of readiness |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1-30 | Product, ICP, positioning, narrative, tools | Delivers the narrative in a role-play |
| Days 31-60 | Discovery, demos, objections, competitors | Runs supervised calls; passes demo certification |
| Days 61-90 | Full deals with coaching | Owns pipeline with call reviews |

## Measuring enablement
| Metric | Source |
|---|---|
| Asset usage in won vs lost deals | Enablement tool and CRM |
| Win rate by stage and segment | CRM |
| Sales cycle length | CRM |
| Ramp time to first deal and quota | Sales operations |
| Rep confidence and usefulness ratings | Quarterly survey |

## Content governance
- One owner for each core asset, with a review date.
- Retire assets unused for two quarters.
- Version names that show the date, not "final-v3".
- A request channel so sellers can ask for missing content.

## Call review routine
Each week, managers and product marketing review two or three recorded calls: one win, one loss and one stalled deal. Note where the narrative landed, which objections came up and which assets helped, then update enablement and coaching from what you hear.

Share call clips of great moments in team meetings so good practice spreads quickly.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Product-led company with a small sales team | Focus on usage-signal plays, short demos and expansion talk tracks |
| Enterprise deals | Add mutual action plans, security and procurement kits, executive briefings |
| Reps resist the new narrative | Pilot with top performers and share their results |
| Content sprawl | Audit, retire unused assets, keep one library |
| New segment | Build segment-specific discovery, demo and proof first |

## Worked example
A B2B security company had a 60-slide deck, a shared drive with 300 files and a nine-month ramp for new reps. Call reviews showed top performers skipped most of the deck, ran discovery on audit deadlines and demoed only the compliance workflow. Enablement rebuilt a 12-slide narrative around passing audits faster, a discovery guide, two use-case demos, an objection guide from win-loss and a 30-60-90 onboarding plan with role-plays. Content moved into the CRM by stage and 240 files were retired. Ramp time fell to around five months, and demo-to-opportunity conversion rose.

## Output format
```
# Sales enablement plan: <company>, <date>
## Diagnosis (where deals stall, top performer patterns, asset usage)
## Sales narrative
## Stage-based content map
## Objection handling and demo flows
## Content organisation and retirement list
## Training and onboarding plan
## Metrics
```

## Common mistakes
- Producing content sellers don't use.
- Feature-tour demos.
- Objection responses without evidence.
- Content scattered across tools.
- Training once, without practice or follow-up.

## Questions to ask before you build
- Where do deals stall, and why?
- What do top performers do differently?
- Which assets do reps use today, and which do they ignore?

## Self-check before you answer
- Is enablement based on win-loss and call evidence?
- Is there one narrative built on positioning?
- Are assets mapped to stages and stored where reps work?
- Are practice and impact measures included?
