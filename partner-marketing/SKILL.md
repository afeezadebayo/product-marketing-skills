# Partner Marketing and Co-Marketing

Partners can reach customers you can't: integration partners whose users need your product, resellers and agencies who advise buyers, marketplaces where customers already shop and complementary companies with the same audience. Done well, partner marketing creates new pipeline at lower cost and adds credibility. Done badly, it produces signed agreements, a logo on a partner page and nothing else.

This skill plans partner marketing: choosing partner types, designing programmes and tiers, joint value propositions, co-marketing campaigns, marketplace listings, partner enablement and measurement of partner-sourced and partner-influenced revenue.

## Operating principles
1. **Shared customers, shared value.** The best partnerships solve a real problem for customers both partners serve.
2. **Fewer, deeper partnerships.** A handful of active partners beats dozens of inactive ones.
3. **Enable partners like sellers.** Partners need positioning, training, demos and proof.
4. **Make co-marketing reciprocal.** Both sides contribute audience, content and effort.
5. **Measure pipeline, not logos.** Partner-sourced and partner-influenced revenue are the real outcomes.

## Partner types
| Type | Value | Typical activities |
|---|---|---|
| Technology and integration partners | Joint value through integration; access to their users | Integration pages, marketplace listings, joint webinars, co-selling |
| Resellers and value-added resellers | Distribution and local relationships | Partner pricing, enablement, deal registration |
| Agencies and consultants | Influence buyer decisions and implement | Referral fees, certification, co-branded content |
| Marketplaces (cloud, app stores) | Buyers purchase where they already spend | Listings, reviews, marketplace-specific offers |
| Co-marketing partners | Shared audience, no integration | Joint research, events, content swaps |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when direct channels plateau, when integrations exist but drive little growth, when entering new markets, when agencies influence your buyers and when building a marketplace presence.

Don't use it for affiliate programmes focused on consumer coupons (the Performance Marketing skills) or for legal contract terms.

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Current partners, agreements and results.
2. Integrations and their usage.
3. ICP and where those customers already buy or seek advice.
4. Sales motion and whether partners can resell or refer.
5. Budget and team capacity for partner management.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Find partner fit
Which companies serve your ICP, integrate with your product or influence your buyers? Score them on audience overlap, strategic fit and willingness to invest.

### Step 2. Design the programme
Partner types, tiers (for example registered, certified, premier), benefits, requirements and commercial terms.

### Step 3. Build joint value propositions
For each priority partner: the shared customer problem, what each product contributes and the combined outcome.

### Step 4. Enable partners
Partner-facing positioning, demo environment, training, certification, sales materials and deal registration.

### Step 5. Plan co-marketing
Joint webinars, integration launch campaigns, co-authored research, case studies with shared customers, event presence and content swaps.

### Step 6. Optimise marketplace listings
Clear descriptions, screenshots, reviews, integration documentation and offers suited to the marketplace.

### Step 7. Co-sell
Account mapping (where both partners have customers or prospects), joint account plans and referral processes.

### Step 8. Measure
Partner-sourced pipeline and revenue, partner-influenced deals, integration adoption, partner activity and partner satisfaction.

## Joint value proposition template
```
Shared customer: <who>
Problem: <what they struggle with>
Partner A contributes: <capability>
Partner B contributes: <capability>
Combined outcome: <result, with proof>
Call to action: <how customers start>
```

## Partner tiers example
| Tier | Requirements | Benefits |
|---|---|---|
| Registered | Signed agreement, basic training | Listing, referral fee |
| Certified | Trained staff, two customer implementations | Higher margin, co-marketing funds, leads |
| Premier | Revenue target, dedicated staff | Joint planning, top placement, executive access |

## Partner fit scorecard
| Criterion | Question | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Audience overlap | How many of our ICP accounts are their customers? | High |
| Product fit | Does an integration or joint offer solve a real problem? | High |
| Willingness to invest | Will they commit people, content and promotion? | High |
| Reach | Size and engagement of their audience | Medium |
| Brand fit | Would customers trust the combination? | Medium |
| Ease of working together | Speed, decision-making, contacts | Medium |
Score partners from 1 to 5 per criterion and focus on the top three to five.

## Co-marketing campaign ideas
| Idea | Effort | Typical outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Joint webinar with a shared customer | Medium | Qualified leads from both audiences |
| Integration launch campaign | Medium | Adoption among both user bases |
| Co-authored research report | High | Coverage, links and leads |
| Shared customer case study | Medium | Proof for both sales teams |
| Newsletter or blog swaps | Low | Awareness |

## Measuring partner impact
| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Partner-sourced pipeline | Opportunities created by partner referrals or registrations |
| Partner-influenced revenue | Closed deals where a partner was involved |
| Integration adoption | Share of customers using the partner integration |
| Retention with integration | Retention of customers using it versus not |
| Partner activity | Trained staff, campaigns run, deals registered |

## Account mapping
Share account lists securely (for example through a partner ecosystem platform) to find overlap: shared customers for expansion and joint references, and your prospects who are their customers for warm introductions. Agree rules on how introductions happen and who owns each relationship.

## Rules of engagement
Define who owns a deal when a partner and direct sales meet the same account, how deal registration works, how long a registration lasts and how disputes are resolved. Clear rules prevent channel conflict and keep partners motivated.

Review each partnership quarterly against its plan and adjust investment accordingly.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Many inactive partners | Focus on the top few; review or retire the rest |
| Large partner with little attention for you | Start with a joint customer story and a specific campaign |
| Channel conflict with direct sales | Clear rules of engagement and deal registration |
| Marketplace sales growing | Treat the marketplace as a channel with its own listing optimisation and offers |
| International partners | Combine with the International SEO and GTM skills |

## Worked example
A HR software company had 40 integration partners listed but almost no partner-sourced pipeline. Analysis showed three payroll providers shared most customers and prospects. The company focused on those three: built joint value propositions around error-free payroll data, ran co-branded webinars, created joint customer case studies, trained the payroll providers' account managers and set up account mapping. Within two quarters, partner-sourced pipeline grew substantially, and deals involving integrations closed faster with higher retention.

## Common mistakes
- Signing many partners and activating none.
- Partner pages with logos but no joint value.
- No enablement for partner sellers.
- One-sided co-marketing.
- Measuring partners by count, not revenue.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- Which partners share your best customers?
- What can each side offer the other's customers?
- How will partner-sourced and influenced revenue be tracked?

## Self-check before you answer
- Are partners chosen by audience overlap and fit?
- Is there a joint value proposition per priority partner?
- Are partners enabled like sellers?
- Are revenue-based metrics defined?
